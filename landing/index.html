Why this exists

Documents are scattered

Statutes, rules, fee schedules and examination guidelines sit in different PDFs, each running to hundreds of pages.

Chatbots guess

A general model will happily invent a section number. In a legal question, a confident wrong answer is worse than no answer.

Answers must be checkable

Every response here points at the exact passage it came from, so you can read the original before you rely on it.

How it works

  1. Ingest. PDFs are extracted, cleaned and split into overlapping chunks, each tagged with its source and page.
  2. Embed. A local multilingual model turns every chunk into a vector that captures meaning, not just keywords.
  3. Retrieve. Your question becomes a vector too; the closest chunks come back from the vector database.
  4. Ground. Only those passages are sent to the language model, which must answer from them and cite them.
  5. Refuse. If nothing is close enough, the assistant says the documents do not cover it instead of guessing.

What it does

Cited answers

Each claim carries a document name and page number, plus a similarity score you can inspect.

Honest refusals

A relevance threshold stops the model from answering outside the knowledge base.

Multilingual questions

Ask in Hindi, Tamil or English; the multilingual embedding model matches against the English text.

Usage analytics

Every query is logged with latency and sources used, feeding a Power BI dashboard on real usage.
